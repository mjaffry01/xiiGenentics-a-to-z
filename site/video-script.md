# Genetics, one idea at a time

A seven-part video script for Class 12 and CPMT. Each part is about four minutes. Record it from the site at `site/index.html`, or animate the same moves in an editor. The pictures already move when a lesson opens. If the viewer has asked the computer to reduce motion, the pictures stay still and the words carry the lesson.

Voice: one teacher, unhurried, as if talking to a student who is seeing the idea for the first time. Do not read the green box aloud in the early parts. Save the examination sentence for the last ten seconds of each part.

On-screen text is short. The voice says the rest.

## Part 1. A mango seed

Lessons: A mango seed grows a mango. Same family, different faces. The body follows a message.

Open on the first lesson. The mango seed appears, the line draws toward a mango tree, then a neem seed arrives and is crossed out.

Narration. A mango seed grows a mango. An elephant's baby is an elephant. That sameness has a name. Heredity. It means passed on. What is passed on is not a tiny finished elephant. It is the information for building one.

Cut to the three figures of different height. Narration. Brothers and sisters are the same kind, and they are not photocopies. That difference is variation. People kept the seeds and the calves they liked. That choosing is artificial selection. The Sahiwal cow is one result.

Cut to the two cards, paint and allele. Narration. Mix red paint into white and you cannot get the red back. A hidden character can disappear for a generation and come back unchanged. So the message stays in pieces. A piece is a gene.

Hold card, ten seconds. On screen: 14 true-breeding varieties. Seven characters. 1856 to 1863. Trichomes were not one of them.

## Part 2. Two copies, then the quiet one returns

Lessons: two copies, one copy louder, the quiet copy comes back, the test cross, the back cross.

Animation. Two letter cards, T and t, meet as Tt. Then the Punnett cells arrive one after another: TT, Tt, Tt, and last the dwarf tt.

Narration. You carry two copies of each gene, one from each parent. The letters are the genotype. The look is the phenotype. Use one letter for one gene. T and t belong together. T and d do not.

When the copies disagree, one look can hide the other. True-breeding tall crossed with true-breeding dwarf gave only tall children. The dwarf copy was silent, not destroyed. Tall is dominant. Dwarf is recessive.

Let those tall plants pollinate themselves. In a large count, about three in four are tall and one in four is dwarf. None are medium. A Tt plant makes T gametes and t gametes equally. The meetings are TT, Tt, Tt, and tt.

A tall plant might be TT or Tt. Cross it with tt. All tall children means the unknown was TT. Half dwarf means it was Tt. That is a test cross. A back cross is wider: the first generation crossed with either parent. The test cross is the back cross that uses only the recessive parent.

Hold card. On screen: F2 genotypes are 1 tall homozygous, 2 tall heterozygous, 1 dwarf. The 3 to 1 ratio is the look, not the letters. Dominance explains the first generation. Segregation explains the return.

## Part 3. When the simple ratio breaks

Lessons: two characters, sixteenths, neither copy simply wins, many genes or one gene.

Animation. Four gamete cards appear, YR, Yr, yR, yr. Then the sixteen boxes. Then three flowers, red, pink, white.

Narration. Yellow is dominant to green. Round is dominant to wrinkled. A plant heterozygous for both makes four gametes in equal number. The next generation is about 9, 3, 3, 1. That is the two 3-to-1 ratios multiplied. Nine sixteenths is 56.25 percent. Three sixteenths is 18.75 percent. For pods, green is dominant to yellow, and inflated is dominant to constricted. Yellow inflated pods are a 3-out-of-16 class, not the 9.

Now the exceptions. A red snapdragon crossed with white gives pink. Pink selfed gives 1 red, 2 pink, 1 white. The letters are still 1, 2, 1. Segregation did not fail. Pink crossed with red gives only red and pink.

Blood groups. I^A and I^B each place a sugar. Allele i places none. Together, I^A and I^B both show. That is co-dominance. Three alleles exist in the population, and one person still carries only two. Group O is ii, so a parent who is I^A I^B cannot have a child of group O. I^A i with I^B i gives group O one time in four.

The starch gene in the pea is the warning. For seed shape, round wins. For grain size, the heterozygote is in between. Same alleles. That is pleiotropy, not multiple alleles. Skin colour in the textbook model is the other way: several genes add. That pattern is non-Mendelian.

Hold card. ABO is dominance, co-dominance, and multiple alleles. It is not incomplete dominance, and it is not polygenic.

## Part 4. Chromosomes, neighbours, and sex

Lessons: chromosomes, linkage, the map, sex.

Animation. Two chromosome pairs. One coloured bar slides from one partner to the other, and back. Then the map line draws and the letters a, c, b, d pop on in that order.

Narration. Genes sit on chromosomes. Chromosomes occur in pairs, and a gamete receives one of each pair. Different pairs can line up in more than one way. That is independent assortment, seen. Sutton and Boveri proposed this. Morgan tested it in the fruit fly. He did not propose it.

When two genes sit on the same chromosome, parental combinations outnumber the new ones. That sticking is linkage. A new combination is recombination. White and yellow recombined in 1.3 percent of offspring. White and miniature wing, 37.2 percent. Sturtevant, not Morgan, turned those percentages into a map. One percent is one map unit.

Put the largest percent at the two ends. If a to d is 29, c is 5 from a, and b is 9 from d, the order is a, c, b, d. Crossing over, helped by recombinase, stays inside one pair of chromosomes. A gene joins a different linkage group by translocation.

Sex. Every human egg carries X. Half the sperm carry X and half carry Y. The sperm decides. In birds the egg decides, because the female is ZW. Grasshopper males are XO. If some animals of that kind have 23 chromosomes and others 24, the 23 are the males. Honey-bee males are haploid and make sperm by mitosis, not meiosis.

Hold card. A mother's X can go to sons and to daughters. A colour-blind man and a woman with two normal X chromosomes have no colour-blind sons.

## Part 5. Four letters, and the proof

Lessons: the ladder, the three experiments, copying.

Animation. Base pairs appear from top to bottom, and each bond draws across. A to T is a thin bond. G to C is a thick one. Then the three replication rows arrive in order: heavy, medium, then half and half.

Narration. A gene is a stretch of DNA. Adenine pairs with thymine by two bonds. Guanine pairs with cytosine by three. The rails run in opposite directions. If you know one side, you know the other. Purines in both DNA and RNA are adenine and guanine. The sugar in RNA is ribose. The sugar in DNA is deoxyribose. If adenine is 30 percent, thymine is 30, and guanine and cytosine are 20 each.

The ladder is wrapped on histones, which are positive and basic, rich in lysine and arginine, eight in an octamer. DNA is the negative partner. A statement that reverses those charges is wrong. Loose chromatin can be read. Dense chromatin cannot.

How we know. Griffith saw dead smooth bacteria change live rough ones, and did not name the substance. Avery's group showed that DNA did it, and cutting DNA stopped it. Hershey and Chase tagged DNA with phosphorus and protein with sulfur. Only the phosphorus entered. That is the unequivocal proof.

Copying keeps one old strand. After one generation on light nitrogen, every ladder is medium. After two, half are medium and half are light. The copier adds only from the 5-prime end toward the 3-prime end. One new strand grows toward the fork. The other is made in Okazaki fragments and joined by ligase. Ten heavy cells, sixty minutes, twenty minutes a generation: eighty cells, and sixty of them have no heavy strand left.

Hold card. Taylor showed this in the bean Vicia faba. Meselson and Stahl used the bacterium, not the pea.

## Part 6. Reading, closing, and a changed letter

Lessons: transcription and translation, the lac switch, pedigree, mutation, chromosomes extra or missing, fingerprinting.

Animation. The three cards DNA, RNA, and protein arrive in a line, and the arrow between them brightens. Then the repressor block on the operon lifts and sits down again. The changed letter in GUG pulses.

Narration. Transcription copies one strand of one gene into RNA. The template is read. The coding strand matches the new RNA, with U where DNA had T. Do not copy both strands. A promoter, the gene, and a terminator mark the unit. In bacteria, sigma helps the start and rho helps the stop. Those two helpers are bacterial. Do not give them to a eukaryote.

Polymerase I makes the large ribosomal RNAs. Polymerase II makes the messenger precursor. Polymerase III makes transfer RNA, 5S RNA, and the small nuclear RNAs. In a eukaryote, cut out the introns, join the exons, cap the 5-prime end, and add adenines at the 3-prime end. Do this before the message leaves the nucleus.

Read the message three letters at a time. AUG is methionine and the start. It is not also phenylalanine. UAA, UAG, and UGA stop. AAA and AAG are both lysine. The code is specific, and it is degenerate, and it is nearly universal. It is not a palindrome. Transfer RNA is the adapter. The small ribosomal subunit meets the message first. In bacteria, 23S RNA forms the peptide bond. Bacterial ribosomes are 70S. Eukaryotic ribosomes are 80S.

The lac genes stay shut until lactose pulls the repressor off. Gene i makes that repressor all the time. Galactose does not induce the operon. If the repressor cannot bind the inducer, the genes stay unread.

When a cross cannot be set up, draw the family. A filled symbol shows the character. A double line means the couple are relatives. A child affected, with parents clear, is the recessive pattern. Myotonic dystrophy is the dominant example in the book. Haemophilia and colour blindness travel on X. Two sickle-cell carriers have a one-in-four chance of an affected child.

One letter can be enough. GAG becomes GUG, and glutamic acid at position 6 becomes valine. That is qualitative. Thalassemia is quantitative: too little chain. Alpha genes are on chromosome 16. The beta gene is on chromosome 11.

An extra chromosome 21 is Down syndrome. The single palm crease belongs there. Klinefelter syndrome is XXY: mostly male, some breast development, sterile, not the short stature of Down syndrome. Turner syndrome is a single X. No cell plate after division makes a whole extra set. That is polyploidy.

People match at 99.9 percent of bases. Fingerprinting compares repeats. Cut, separate, blot, probe, then the film. Chromosome 1 was finished last, and it carries the most genes in that list.

Hold card. Sequence annotation reads the whole genome first and names functions later. An expressed sequence tag starts from RNA that was actually made.

## Part 7. A crowd of alleles, and deep time

Lessons: Hardy-Weinberg, the five pushes, the long story.

Animation. The three bars of the Hardy-Weinberg widget shift as the slider moves from 50 to 40. Then the three selection curves draw: a hill in the middle, a hill moved to one side, and two hills at the ends. Last, the timeline draws from left to right and the dates appear.

Narration. Leave the person. Count the alleles in the crowd. If p is the fraction of A, and q is the fraction of a, and nothing pushes, AA is p squared, aa is q squared, and Aa is 2pq. If p is 0.4, those three numbers are 0.16, 0.48, and 0.36. A constant gene pool is the quiet baseline. It is not a push. The pushes are migration, chance in a small group, a new letter, a new combination, and unequal success in leaving offspring. The founder effect is that chance, genetic drift.

Selection can keep the middle, move the crowd off the old average, or keep both ends. Fitness means more offspring. Darwin and Wallace saw the small inherited differences. de Vries argued for one large step, saltation, random and directionless. A stretched neck acquired in one life is not passed on.

Same bones, different jobs: homology, divergent evolution. A bird wing and a whale flipper. Same job, different build: analogy, convergent evolution. Penguin and dolphin flippers. Insect and bird wings. Sweet potato and potato. Darwin's finches are adaptive radiation, not a human-made breed. Herbicide-resistant weeds, drug-resistant cells, and breeds of dog are human-made.

The dates. Earth, about 4.5 billion years. Miller's flask: methane, hydrogen, ammonia, water vapour, 800 degrees, sparks, and amino acids formed. Invertebrates near 500 million years ago. Jawless fish near 350. Seaweeds near 320. Dinosaurs gone near 65 million years ago. Homo habilis, then erectus, then Neanderthal, then us. Brains about 650 to 800, then 900, then 1400. Neanderthal used hides and buried the dead. Our species arose in Africa.

Haeckel said an embryo replays adult ancestors. von Baer showed that it does not. Fossils and homologous limbs remain the evidence.

Last line, on screen and spoken. A mango seed still grows a mango. It carries a ladder of four letters, copied, inherited in pairs, usually unchanged, and sometimes changed in frequency across a crowd and across deep time.

## Recording notes

Open the lesson, let the picture finish, then speak. The Punnett cells, the bonds, the replication rows, the repressor, and the timeline are timed to about two seconds. Do not talk over the first appearance. Pause while a cell lands.

Each part ends on a hold card. Those lines are the ones the medical entrance repeats. They are also written under Medical entrance in the lesson, with a question to click.
